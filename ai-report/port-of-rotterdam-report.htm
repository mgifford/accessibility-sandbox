  <h1>Digital Accessibility Remediation Report: Port of Rotterdam</h1>

  <p><strong>Prepared for:</strong> The Port of Rotterdam Authority<br>

  <strong>Date:</strong> September 2026<br>

  <strong>Subject:</strong> Web Accessibility Compliance, Barrier Analysis, and Strategic Remediation Roadmap</p>


  <h2>1. Executive Summary</h2>

  <p>The Port of Rotterdam Authority is committed to ensuring its digital platforms are accessible to all users, in alignment with the European Union Web Accessibility Directive (Directive 2016/2102) and the Dutch Government Digital Accessibility Decree. Based on the independent audit conducted on January 16, 2026, the primary domain (portofrotterdam.com) and associated subdomains currently hold a <strong>Status B: Partially Compliant</strong> rating against the EN 301 549 / WCAG 2.1 Level AA standards.</p>

  <p>While the Port Authority is transparent about its current barriers and has provided alternatives (phone and email support), significant remediation is required across both technical infrastructure and content management. This report outlines the specific barriers identified, delineates responsibilities between internal content teams and external development partners, and provides a structured roadmap to achieve full compliance (Status A) by the stated deadline of <strong>December 31, 2027</strong>.</p>


  <h2>2. Regulatory Context</h2>

  <p>Under the Government Digital Accessibility Decree, public sector bodies must ensure their websites and mobile applications meet digital accessibility standards. The framework is defined as follows:</p>

  <ul>

    <li><strong>Standard:</strong> EN 301 549 (equivalent to WCAG 2.1 Level A and AA).</li>

    <li><strong>Current Status:</strong> Partially Compliant. The Port maintains a valid accessibility statement, possesses a recent audit, and has a plan, but technical deviations remain.</li>

    <li><strong>Enforcement Target:</strong> Attain Status A (Fully Compliant) by the December 31, 2027 deadline documented in the Port's official accessibility statement.</li>

  </ul>

  <h2>4. Division of Responsibilities</h2>


  <h3>4.1. Internal Port Authority Team (Content Remediation)</h3>

  <ul>

    <li><strong>Alternative Text (SC 1.1.1):</strong> Write accurate, descriptive <code>alt</code> text for all informative images and nullify (<code>alt=""</code>) decorative images.</li>

    <li><strong>Multimedia (SC 1.2.2, 1.2.3, 1.2.5):</strong> Audit existing pre-recorded video assets. Outsource or generate closed captions (.vtt/.srt) and text transcripts/audio descriptions.</li>

    <li><strong>Heading Structure (SC 2.4.6):</strong> Ensure all rich-text content follows a strict hierarchical heading structure (H1, H2, H3) without skipping levels.</li>

    <li><strong>Link Phrasing (SC 2.4.4):</strong> Eliminate ambiguous links ("Click Here", "Read More"). Ensure link text accurately describes its destination.</li>

    <li><strong>Language Attributes (SC 3.1.2):</strong> Ensure multi-language text within a page is tagged with language attributes.</li>

  </ul>


  <h3>4.2. External Development Partner (Technical Infrastructure)</h3>

  <ul>

    <li><strong>Keyboard Operability & Focus (SC 2.1.1, 2.4.3, 2.4.7):</strong> Ensure all interactive UI elements are operable via the <code>Tab</code> key and possess a visible CSS focus ring.</li>

    <li><strong>Semantic HTML & ARIA (SC 1.3.1, 4.1.2):</strong> Ensure custom widgets broadcast Name, Role, and Value to assistive technologies.</li>

    <li><strong>Responsive Design (SC 1.4.10, 1.4.12):</strong> Refactor CSS to allow zooming up to 400% without two-dimensional scrolling.</li>

    <li><strong>Color & Contrast (SC 1.4.1, 1.4.3, 1.4.11):</strong> Update CSS variables to ensure text meets minimum 4.5:1 contrast ratios.</li>

    <li><strong>Motion Control (SC 2.2.2, 1.4.13):</strong> Implement global pause/play controls for auto-advancing carousels.</li>

  </ul>
