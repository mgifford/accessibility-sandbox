Digital Accessibility Remediation Report: Port of Rotterdam

Prepared for: The Port of Rotterdam Authority
Date: September 2026
Subject: Web Accessibility Compliance, Barrier Analysis, and Strategic Remediation Roadmap

1. Executive Summary

The Port of Rotterdam Authority is committed to ensuring its digital platforms are accessible to all users, in alignment with the European Union Web Accessibility Directive (Directive 2016/2102) and the Dutch Government Digital Accessibility Decree. Based on the independent audit conducted on January 16, 2026, the primary domain (portofrotterdam.com) and associated subdomains currently hold a Status B: Partially Compliant rating against the EN 301 549 / WCAG 2.1 Level AA standards.

While the Port Authority is transparent about its current barriers and has provided alternatives (phone and email support), significant remediation is required across both technical infrastructure and content management. This report outlines the specific barriers identified, delineates responsibilities between internal content teams and external development partners, and provides a structured roadmap to achieve full compliance (Status A) by the stated deadline of December 31, 2027.

2. Regulatory Context

Under the Government Digital Accessibility Decree, public sector bodies must ensure their websites and mobile applications meet digital accessibility standards. The framework is defined as follows:

  • Standard: EN 301 549 (equivalent to WCAG 2.1 Level A and AA).
  • Current Status: Partially Compliant. The Port maintains a valid accessibility statement, possesses a recent audit, and has a plan, but technical deviations remain.
  • Enforcement Target: Attain Status A (Fully Compliant) by the December 31, 2027 deadline documented in the Port's official accessibility statement.

3. Current State Analysis: Identified Barriers

The independent audit identified numerous deviations across the four pillars of WCAG 2.1: Perceivable, Operable, Understandable, and Robust.

WCAG Category Specific Failures (Level A & AA) Impact on Users
Visual & Non-Text Content SC 1.1.1 (Non-text content), SC 1.4.1 (Use of colour), SC 1.4.3 & 1.4.11 (Contrast), SC 1.4.5 (Images of text) Visually impaired and colorblind users cannot perceive essential information, UI elements, or meaningful images. Screen readers cannot describe missing alt text.
Media & Video SC 1.2.2 (Subtitles), SC 1.2.3 & 1.2.5 (Audio description) Deaf and hard-of-hearing users miss spoken content; blind users miss essential on-screen visual information.
Keyboard & Navigation SC 2.1.1 (Keyboard), SC 2.4.1 (Bypass blocks), SC 2.4.3 (Focus order), SC 2.4.7 (Focus visible) Motor-impaired users utilizing keyboards or switch devices cannot navigate the site, locate their focus indicator, or skip repetitive menus.
Structure & Semantics SC 1.3.1 (Info and relationships), SC 1.3.2 (Meaningful sequence), SC 2.4.6 (Headings and labels), SC 4.1.2 (Name, role, value) Assistive technologies cannot accurately interpret page structures, custom interactive elements, or form labels.
Usability & Layout SC 1.3.5 (Identify input purpose), SC 1.4.10 (Reflow), SC 2.2.2 (Pause, stop, hide) Users with cognitive disabilities cannot use auto-fill for forms; users who zoom in experience broken layouts; users with ADHD cannot pause moving elements.

4. Division of Responsibilities

4.1. Internal Port Authority Team (Content Remediation)

  • Alternative Text (SC 1.1.1): Write accurate, descriptive alt text for all informative images and nullify (alt="") decorative images.
  • Multimedia (SC 1.2.2, 1.2.3, 1.2.5): Audit existing pre-recorded video assets. Outsource or generate closed captions (.vtt/.srt) and text transcripts/audio descriptions.
  • Heading Structure (SC 2.4.6): Ensure all rich-text content follows a strict hierarchical heading structure (H1, H2, H3) without skipping levels.
  • Link Phrasing (SC 2.4.4): Eliminate ambiguous links ("Click Here", "Read More"). Ensure link text accurately describes its destination.
  • Language Attributes (SC 3.1.2): Ensure multi-language text within a page is tagged with language attributes.

4.2. External Development Partner (Technical Infrastructure)

  • Keyboard Operability & Focus (SC 2.1.1, 2.4.3, 2.4.7): Ensure all interactive UI elements are operable via the Tab key and possess a visible CSS focus ring.
  • Semantic HTML & ARIA (SC 1.3.1, 4.1.2): Ensure custom widgets broadcast Name, Role, and Value to assistive technologies.
  • Responsive Design (SC 1.4.10, 1.4.12): Refactor CSS to allow zooming up to 400% without two-dimensional scrolling.
  • Color & Contrast (SC 1.4.1, 1.4.3, 1.4.11): Update CSS variables to ensure text meets minimum 4.5:1 contrast ratios.
  • Motion Control (SC 2.2.2, 1.4.13): Implement global pause/play controls for auto-advancing carousels.

5. Strategic Remediation Roadmap (Target: Dec 31, 2027)

Phase / Timeline Focus Area Key Deliverables
Phase 1: Triage
Q4 2026 - Q1 2027		 High-Impact Blockers
  • Deploy "Skip to Main Content" links globally.
  • Fix CSS focus rings for keyboard users.
  • Address ARIA errors on critical navigation menus.
Phase 2: Structural Fixes
Q2 2027 - Q3 2027		 Visual, Semantics & Media
  • Deploy contrast and CSS reflow fixes.
  • Update form structures (labels and autocomplete).
  • Upload closed captions/transcripts for top 20% of videos.
Phase 3: Deep Remediation
Early Q4 2027		 Content Cleanup & Edge Cases
  • Clean up ambiguous link text and heading structures.
  • Provide WCAG training for internal content editors.
  • Implement automated testing (axe) in CI/CD pipeline.
Phase 4: Validation
Late Q4 2027		 Re-Audit & Certification
  • Engage independent third-party auditor for WCAG 2.1 AA review.
  • Update Accessibility Statement to Status A (Fully Compliant).

6. Conclusion & Next Steps

Achieving full compliance by the end of 2027 requires immediate, synchronized action between the Port of Rotterdam's content managers and external technical partners. Prioritizing high-impact operability blockers first will significantly reduce user exclusion early in the remediation process.
